Now look all the way to the top of the Board of Trade. That aluminum statue on the pyramid roof is Ceres, the Roman goddess of grain, by the sculptor John Storrs. She is about thirty feet tall, and she has no face, only smooth, abstract planes.

For generations, the price of the world's wheat, corn, and soybeans was set in shouting matches in trading pits on this spot. In 1897, a young speculator named Joseph Leiter tried to corner the world's wheat. The meatpacker Philip Armour broke the corner by chartering icebreakers to smash a path through the frozen lakes, and bring his grain south in the middle of winter.

Walk east to State Street, past Mies van der Rohe's black steel towers and Alexander Calder's red Flamingo, and listen for the elevated trains squealing around the Loop.
